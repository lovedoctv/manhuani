**Express Entry** is Canada's main system for skilled workers who want permanent residence. On 16 October 2026, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) announced the biggest change to its scoring in more than a year. From **3 November 2026**, candidates with a job offer or Canadian work experience in a **high-wage occupation** get extra points, and a qualifying job offer no longer needs a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA).

Here's how it works, and what it means if you're applying from Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Zimbabwe or Zambia.

## How Express Entry works

Express Entry covers three programmes:

- **Federal Skilled Worker Program:** the one most people abroad use. You need at least one year of continuous skilled work in the last 10 years, a language score of at least CLB 7, your education assessed, 67 points on the programme's grid and proof of funds.
- **Canadian Experience Class:** for people who already have skilled work experience in Canada.
- **Federal Skilled Trades Program:** for trades such as electricians, welders and plumbers.

You create a profile and get a **Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS)** score out of 1,200. IRCC then holds draws and invites the highest-scoring candidates to apply for permanent residence. Some draws are open to everyone; others are for categories such as health care, skilled trades and education.

## What changes on 3 November 2026

### Points for a job offer in a high-wage occupation

Since March 2025, a job offer gave you **no CRS points** at all. From 3 November 2026, a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation earns points again:

- **100 points** if the occupation's median wage is at least **2 times** the average median wage in Canada.
- **70 points** if it's at least **1.5 times**.
- **50 points** if it's at least **1.3 times**.

The offer must generally be for **full-time, continuous work** in Canada for **at least one year** after you become a permanent resident. It **doesn't need an LMIA**, but IRCC still checks that it's genuine: that the employer is a real, active business, needs the role, can pay you and follows employment law. A job offer that's withdrawn no longer counts.

### Points for Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation

If you already have at least **12 months** of skilled work in Canada in a high-wage occupation, you get **70, 40 or 20** extra points, using the same 2, 1.5 and 1.3 times tiers. These come on top of the normal points for Canadian experience.

You get either the job offer points or the experience points, whichever is higher. They aren't added together. The total for this part of your score is capped at **150 points**, or **140** if your spouse or partner is coming with you.

### Job offers for the Federal Skilled Worker and Trades programmes

A temporary policy also removes the LMIA requirement for job offers used to qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades programmes, for candidates invited on or after 3 November 2026 who meet its conditions.

## Which jobs count as high-wage

The list goes by your **occupation's code** (NOC) and the national median wage for that occupation, **not your own salary**. It covers sectors such as engineering, technology, health care, education, management and skilled trades. Software developers, engineers, data scientists, registered nurses, accountants and electricians are among the occupations on it.

Find your NOC code first, then check which tier it's in on [IRCC's high-wage occupations list](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/check-score/high-wage.html).

## What it means if you're applying from Africa

- **The Canadian experience points won't help you yet**, because they need 12 months of work inside Canada.
- **A genuine job offer can now add 50 to 100 points**, without your employer going through the LMIA process. That makes it easier for Canadian employers to hire you in a high-wage occupation.
- **More points don't guarantee an invitation.** Each draw has its own cut-off score.
- **Never pay for a job offer.** Expect a wave of people selling "LMIA-free job offers". IRCC checks every offer, and lying on an application can get you banned from applying for 5 years. Here's [how to spot fake job and visa offers](/blog/spot-fake-job-abroad-visa-offers/).

## What to do now

**If you're already in the pool and have a job offer**, update your profile with all the job offer details **before 3 November 2026**. IRCC will recalculate everyone's scores, which can take up to two weeks, and it's pausing invitation rounds while it does.

**If you're not in the pool yet**, get the basics ready:

1. Get an **Educational Credential Assessment** for your degree. See [how the ECA and WES work](/blog/canada-eca-wes-express-entry/).
2. Take an **English test IRCC accepts**: IELTS General Training, CELPIP-General or PTE Core. The Duolingo English Test doesn't count for Express Entry. See [Duolingo or IELTS](/blog/duolingo-english-test-vs-ielts/).
3. Check you have the **proof of funds** below.
4. Work out your NOC code and whether it's on the high-wage list.

## Proof of funds

Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades candidates must show they have at least this much, in Canadian dollars:

| Family members | Funds you need |
|---|---|
| 1 | CAD 15,263 |
| 2 | CAD 19,001 |
| 3 | CAD 23,360 |
| 4 | CAD 28,362 |
| 5 | CAD 32,168 |

You don't need proof of funds if you're applying through the Canadian Experience Class, or if you're authorised to work in Canada and have a valid job offer. These are the amounts on IRCC's page in October 2026, and IRCC updates them every year.
