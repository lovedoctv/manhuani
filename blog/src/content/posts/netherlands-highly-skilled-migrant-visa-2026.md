The Netherlands has one of Europe's simplest routes for skilled workers: the **highly skilled migrant** permit, often called the *kennismigrant* scheme. Your employer applies for you, the main test is your salary, and your partner can come with you.

Here's how it works in 2026 for people from Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Zimbabwe and Zambia.

## Who can use it

You need:

- a job offer and employment contract from an employer the IND has approved as a **recognised sponsor**. Only these employers can apply for this permit, and they're listed in the IND's public register of recognised sponsors;
- a salary that meets the **2026 threshold** for your age, shown below;
- a salary that's in line with the **market rate** for the job.

The IND's main tests are the employer and the salary, so check both before you spend time on an application.

## Salary thresholds for 2026

These are gross monthly amounts, before tax and **without holiday pay**:

| Who | Minimum gross salary a month |
|---|---|
| 30 or older | €5,942 |
| Under 30 | €4,357 |
| Reduced criterion (recent graduates) | €3,122 |

The amounts change every **1 January**, and the one that counts is the amount on the day your employer applies. If you were under 30 when you first got the permit, the lower amount still applies at your extension with the same employer. If you change employer, your age when the new contract starts decides which amount applies.

## The lower salary for recent graduates

The reduced amount of **€3,122** applies if you have, or qualify for, the Netherlands' **orientation year** permit for highly educated people, and your employer applies within **3 years** of your graduation. For graduates from outside the Netherlands, that generally means:

- a **master's, PhD or post-master's** degree,
- from a university in the **top 200** of the general or subject rankings of at least two of Times Higher Education, QS and ShanghaiRanking,
- earned within the last **3 years**.

Graduates of Dutch universities and Erasmus Mundus joint master's programmes also qualify. The IND asks for a Nuffic evaluation of foreign diplomas. Ask the IND if you're unsure whether your degree counts.

## EU Blue Card in the Netherlands

The Netherlands also issues the **EU Blue Card**, which needs a higher education degree. In 2026 it needs a salary of **€5,942** a month, or **€4,754** under the reduced criterion. Germany's Blue Card asks for much less. Compare it in our [EU Blue Card Germany guide](/blog/germany-eu-blue-card-2026/).

## How to apply

Your employer does most of the work:

1. Your employer checks you meet the conditions and collects your documents.
2. **Your employer applies** to the IND, usually online, and pays the fee.
3. The IND decides.
4. If it's approved, you book an appointment at the Dutch embassy or consulate for your **MVV** (an entry visa sticker) and travel to the Netherlands.
5. You collect your residence permit in the Netherlands.

You may need a **TB test** at the local health service (GGD) within 3 months of getting your permit. If the decision says so, you can start work before you have the permit card, but you must book your biometrics appointment within 2 weeks of your employer receiving the decision.

## Fees in 2026

- **€423** for the highly skilled migrant permit.
- **€254** for a partner.
- **€85** for a child under 18.

## How long it lasts

The permit lasts as long as your contract, up to **5 years**, and your employer can apply to extend it. After 5 years you can apply for **permanent residence**, if you meet the other conditions.

If you lose your job, you have **3 months** to find a new one with a recognised sponsor. If you've had the permit for at least 2 years and became unemployed on or after 22 May 2026, you get up to **6 months**.

## Bringing your family

Your partner and children can apply through your employer at the same time as you. Partners of highly skilled migrants are **allowed to work** in the Netherlands.

## Where to find employers

Search the IND's register of recognised sponsors for companies in your field, then look for their jobs on their own sites and on LinkedIn. Technology, engineering, finance and research employers are among those that hire from abroad.

Never pay anyone to "arrange" a Dutch work permit. Only the employer can apply. Read [how to spot fake job and visa offers](/blog/spot-fake-job-abroad-visa-offers/).
