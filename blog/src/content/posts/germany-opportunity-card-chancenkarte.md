Most work visas need a job offer first. Germany's Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) works the other way round: it lets you move to Germany for up to a year to look for a job, and work part-time while you search.

It's open to people from any country outside the EU, including Nigerians, Ghanaians, Kenyans, South Africans, Zimbabweans and Zambians.

## Who qualifies

There are two ways in.

**1. Your qualification is fully recognised in Germany.** If a German authority has already recognised your degree or vocational qualification as equal to a German one, you qualify without points.

**2. You score at least 6 points.** For this route you need:

- a university degree, or at least 2 years of vocational training, that is recognised in your home country,
- German at A1 level or English at B2 level, and
- at least 6 points.

| What you have | Points |
|---|---|
| Qualification partly recognised in Germany | 4 |
| Qualification in a shortage occupation | 1 |
| Work experience: 2+ years in the last 5 | 2 |
| Work experience: 5+ years in the last 7 | 3 |
| German A2 / B1 / B2 or higher | 1 / 2 / 3 |
| English C1 or higher (or native speaker) | 1 |
| Age under 35 | 2 |
| Age 35 to 40 | 1 |
| 6+ months' legal stay in Germany in the last 5 years (not as a tourist) | 1 |
| Spouse or partner who also qualifies for the card | 1 |

For example, a 30-year-old nurse with 5 years' experience and German B1 scores 3 + 2 + 2 = 7 points.

Source: [Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/opportunity-card/job-search).

## How much money you need

You must show you can support yourself while you search. The usual way is a **blocked account** holding **€1,091 for each month** of your stay in 2026. For a full year, that's €13,092.

The other option is a declaration of commitment, where someone in Germany formally agrees to cover your costs.

## What you can do with it

- Stay in Germany for up to 1 year.
- Work part-time, up to **20 hours a week**.
- Do trial work for up to 2 weeks per employer.
- Switch to a residence permit for skilled work in Germany once you find a qualifying job, without going home first.

## How to apply

1. Check your points or get your qualification recognised.
2. Open a blocked account and arrange health insurance.
3. Book a national visa appointment at the German embassy or consulate for your country.
4. Pay the **€75** visa fee and submit your documents.

The German Embassy in Accra lists the fee and documents on its [visa information page](https://accra.diplo.de/gh-en/service/-visainformation/2656920-2656920). Check the page for your own country before your appointment, as document lists can differ.

## Tips

- Learning German is the biggest advantage you can give yourself. It earns points for the card, and most jobs in Germany need it.
- Start applying for jobs before you travel, so you use your year well.
- Nurses and care professionals should look at Germany's recognition process for health jobs, which is separate from the Opportunity Card.
