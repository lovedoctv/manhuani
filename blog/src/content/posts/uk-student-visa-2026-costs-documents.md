The UK is still one of the most popular places for students from Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Zimbabwe and Zambia to study. A one-year master's gets you a degree fast, and the Graduate visa lets you stay and work afterwards.

But the **Student visa** has costs people underestimate. Here's what you'll pay, what you must show and how to get it right first time.

## What you need before you apply

- An **unconditional offer** from a university that's a licensed student sponsor. The university then gives you a **Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)** number.
- **English at level B2** for a degree course. Your university can assess this itself, or ask you for an approved test such as IELTS for UKVI.
- **Money** for your fees and living costs, held for 28 days (see below).
- A **TB test certificate** (see below).
- A valid **passport**.

## What it costs

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Visa fee, applying from outside the UK | £558 |
| Immigration health surcharge | £776 a year |
| Each family member who comes with you | £558 visa fee plus £776 a year |

The surcharge depends on the **length of your visa**, not your course. A visa for more than a year but no more than 18 months costs **£1,164** in surcharge; a visa for more than 18 months but under 2 years costs **£1,552**. A one-year master's usually gets a visa of around 16 to 17 months, because it includes extra time after the course ends, so most master's students pay £1,164.

You also pay for the TB test and, if your university asks for it, an English test.

## How much money you must show

You need to show your **unpaid course fees for the first year**, plus living costs of:

- **£1,529 a month** if you'll study in **London**, for up to 9 months: **£13,761**.
- **£1,171 a month** anywhere else in the UK, for up to 9 months: **£10,539**.

If your family comes with you, add **£845 a month** for each person in London, or **£680** elsewhere, for up to 9 months.

The money must sit in your account for at least **28 days in a row**, and the end of that 28-day period must be within **31 days** of the day you apply. Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Zimbabwe and Zambia aren't on the UK's reduced-evidence list, so you must send the bank statements with your application.

You don't need to show living costs if you've already lived in the UK on a valid visa for 12 months or more. Our [proof of funds guide](/blog/proof-of-funds-student-visa-uk-canada-germany/) explains which statements and sponsor letters UKVI accepts.

## The TB test

If you're applying from **Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Zimbabwe or Zambia**, you need a tuberculosis test from a clinic approved by the UK Home Office. Book it early, because you need the certificate before you apply, and only approved clinics count.

## Can you bring your family?

Only some students can:

- students on a **PhD, other doctorate or research-based master's** course,
- **government-sponsored** students on a course longer than 6 months.

If you're starting a **taught master's** or an undergraduate degree, you **can't bring** your partner or children on your Student visa.

## When to apply and how long it takes

- From outside the UK, you can apply up to **6 months** before your course starts.
- You give your fingerprints and photo at a visa application centre.
- You'll usually get a decision within **3 weeks**.

Apply as soon as you have your CAS and your money has been in your account for 28 days. September intakes are the busiest time.

## Working while you study

At degree level you can work up to **20 hours a week** during term time, and full-time in the holidays. Many students use this for living costs, but you can't count future earnings as your proof of funds.

## After you graduate

The **Graduate visa** lets you stay and work after your degree. It's getting shorter: people who apply from 1 January 2027 get 18 months instead of 2 years. See [what the Graduate visa change means for you](/blog/uk-graduate-visa-apply-before-2027/), and the [Skilled Worker visa rules](/blog/uk-skilled-worker-visa-2026-rules/) if you want to stay longer.
