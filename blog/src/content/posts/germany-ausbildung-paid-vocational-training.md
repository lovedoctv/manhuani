An **Ausbildung** is Germany's vocational training. You sign a training contract with a German company, learn the job there, and spend part of the week at a vocational school. You're **paid a training allowance every month**, and at the end you get a German qualification.

It's one of the best routes to Germany if you don't have a university degree. Training programmes usually last 2 to 3.5 years. Common fields include nursing and elderly care, electrical work, plumbing and heating, hotels and restaurants, and logistics.

## What you need for the training visa

- A **training contract** or confirmed training place with a German company.
- German at **B1 level**, unless the company has tested your German itself.
- Proof you can **support yourself**. For company training in 2026, that's at least **€822 net (€1,048 gross) a month**. If your training pay is lower, ask the German embassy how to show the difference, for example with a blocked account.
- Valid **health insurance**.
- Your passport and original documents.

For school-based training, where you train at a school instead of a company, you must show at least **€959 net a month**. You can do this with a blocked account, a scholarship or a formal Declaration of Commitment from someone in Germany.

The Federal Employment Agency also checks that you'll be trained and paid on the same terms as German trainees. The embassy arranges this, so you don't need to do anything.

**Fees:** the visa costs €75, paid in local currency. Your residence permit in Germany can cost up to €100 more.

## No training place yet? Use the search visa

Germany has a separate visa to **come and look for a training place**. You need:

- to be **35 or younger**,
- German at **B1 level** or higher,
- a school-leaving certificate that lets you go to university in Germany or in your home country,
- a blocked account with at least **€1,091 a month** (2026 figure), a scholarship or a Declaration of Commitment,
- health insurance.

You can stay up to **9 months** while you search. You may work up to 20 hours a week and do up to 2 weeks of trial work with companies.

## During your training

Your residence permit lasts as long as your training. You can take a **side job of up to 20 hours a week**, as long as it isn't part of your training.

## After you qualify

- You can stay **up to 18 months** to find a job in your field. For care and health assistant training, it's up to 12 months, which can be extended once by 6 months.
- While you look, you can take any job to pay your bills.
- After **2 years of work** following your training, you may be able to get a **settlement permit**, which is permanent residence, if you meet the other conditions.

## How to find a training place

1. Learn German to B1. Start now, because it takes most people many months.
2. Search the Federal Employment Agency's job portal and the Make it in Germany job listings for "Ausbildung" places that accept international applicants.
3. Apply directly to the companies with a German CV and cover letter.
4. Once you have a contract, book your visa appointment at the German embassy or consulate.

Real German companies don't charge you for a training place. Be careful with agents who ask for large fees to "guarantee" an Ausbildung. Read [how to spot fake job abroad offers](/blog/spot-fake-job-abroad-visa-offers/).
