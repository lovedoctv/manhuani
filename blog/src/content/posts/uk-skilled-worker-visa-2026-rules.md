The UK Skilled Worker visa is still open to people from every African country, but it's much harder to qualify than it was two years ago. Three rules changed: the type of job, the salary, and the English level.

If you were planning to go as a care worker, read our guide on [what replaced the UK care visa](/blog/uk-care-worker-visa-closed-alternatives/) first, because that route has closed to people applying from abroad.

## The job must be degree level

Since 22 July 2025, the job must be at RQF level 6, which is roughly degree level. Medium-skilled jobs only qualify if they are on one of two lists:

- the **Immigration Salary List**, or
- the **Temporary Shortage List**.

The Temporary Shortage List is time-limited to the end of 2026 unless the Migration Advisory Committee recommends keeping it. Workers who come through a job on that list can't bring dependants.

## The salary must be at least £41,700

The general minimum rose from £38,700 to **£41,700** on 22 July 2025. If the going rate for your job is higher, you must be paid the going rate.

A lower minimum, from £33,400, applies in some cases, for example to some new entrants. [GOV.UK lists the cases](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/your-job).

Doctors, nurses and other health professionals usually apply through the Health and Care Worker visa instead, which has a lower £25,000 floor or the NHS pay scale.

## English is now B2

From 8 January 2026, Skilled Worker and Health and Care Worker applicants must prove English at **B2** level, up from B1. Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Zimbabwe and Zambia are not exempt, even though English is an official language.

With IELTS for UKVI, B2 means **5.5 in each of the four skills**. See [IELTS fees in Nigeria](/blog/ielts-fee-nigeria-2026/) for what the test costs.

## Other changes to know

- **Graduate visa:** if you study in the UK and apply for the Graduate visa from 1 January 2027, you get 18 months instead of 2 years. PhD graduates still get 3 years.
- **Visa brake:** the restrictions announced on 26 March 2026 apply to Afghanistan, Cameroon, Myanmar and Sudan only.

## How to apply

1. Find a job with an employer that holds a UK sponsor licence. You can check any employer on the [register of licensed sponsors](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/register-of-licensed-sponsors-workers).
2. Your employer assigns you a certificate of sponsorship.
3. Take an approved English test, such as IELTS for UKVI.
4. Apply online on GOV.UK and attend your biometrics appointment.

## Watch out for fees

Your employer must pay its own Immigration Skills Charge. GOV.UK warns sponsors that their licence may be revoked if they ask a sponsored worker to pay it or any costs linked to their application. Anyone asking you for money for a "certificate of sponsorship" is a red flag.
