UK Graduate visa: apply by 31 December 2026 to keep the 2-year stay

Updated 25 Oct 2026 · Facts checked 25 Oct 2026

Stay
2 years if you apply by 31 December 2026, 18 months from 1 January 2027 (3 years after a PhD)
Fee
£937, plus a health surcharge of £1,035 a year
Decision
Usually within 8 weeks
Extension
Not possible, but you can switch to a Skilled Worker visa
Deadline
31 Dec 2026 65 days left
Official page: GOV.UK

If you’re finishing a bachelor’s or master’s degree in the UK, the date you apply for your Graduate visa now matters:

  • Apply on or before 31 December 2026 and you get 2 years to live and work in the UK.
  • Apply from 1 January 2027 and you get 18 months.
  • PhD and other doctoral graduates keep 3 years either way.

The new rule depends on the date you submit your application, not the date your course started.

Who can apply

You can apply if all of these are true:

  • You’re in the UK on a Student visa (or an older Tier 4 (General) student visa), and it hasn’t expired yet.
  • You studied a UK bachelor’s, master’s or PhD, or another eligible course such as a PGCE, PGDE or some law courses.
  • Your university has told the Home Office you completed your course. You don’t have to wait for your graduation ceremony or certificate.
  • Your university is listed as a “Student Sponsor - Track Record” on the Home Office register.

You also need enough time in the UK:

  • If your course was longer than 12 months, you must have studied in the UK for at least 12 months.
  • If your course was 12 months or shorter, you must have studied in the UK for the whole course.

To beat the 31 December cut-off, ask your international student office when they’ll report your completion to the Home Office. You can’t apply until they do.

How much it costs

  • Application fee: £937.
  • Immigration Health Surcharge: £1,035 for each year of the visa, so £2,070 for a 2-year visa.

Your partner and children each pay their own application fee and surcharge.

Documents you need

  • Your passport.
  • The CAS reference number from your Student visa application. Your university can find it if you’ve lost it.
  • Your biometric residence permit (BRP), if you have one. You can use it until 24 months after the expiry date on the card, or until 31 December 2026, whichever comes first.
  • A letter from your scholarship or sponsor approving the application, if they paid both your course fees and your living costs in the last 12 months.
  • Certified translations of anything not in English or Welsh.

On a scholarship that requires you to go home, such as Chevening, you’re expected to return for at least 2 years after your award. Read your scholarship’s rules before you apply. See what happens after you apply for Chevening.

Your partner and children

Your partner and children can stay with you, but they usually must already be your dependants on your Student visa. A child born in the UK while you’re on a Student or Graduate visa is the main exception.

New dependants can’t join you on a Graduate visa. They may be able to join you later if you switch to a work visa such as the Skilled Worker visa.

How to apply

  1. Check that your university has reported your completion.
  2. Apply online on GOV.UK before your Student visa expires.
  3. Prove your identity with the UK Immigration: ID Check app, or by giving your fingerprints and photo at a UKVCAS service point. You’ll be told which one to use.
  4. Don’t travel outside the UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands or the Isle of Man until you get a decision, or your application will be withdrawn.
  5. You’ll usually get a decision within 8 weeks. If you’re approved, you get an eVisa, which you view in your UKVI account.

What you can do on a Graduate visa

You can work in most jobs, look for work, be self-employed and volunteer. You can’t claim most public benefits or work as a professional sportsperson.

You can’t extend a Graduate visa. Use the time to find an employer who can sponsor you, then switch to a Skilled Worker visa before it runs out. Read the UK Skilled Worker visa rules for 2026 to check the salary you’ll need.
