EU Blue Card Germany 2026: salary rules, shortage jobs and fast permanent residence

Updated 27 Oct 2026 · Facts checked 27 Oct 2026

Salary
€50,700 a year, or €45,934.20 in shortage jobs and for recent graduates
Degree
A German or comparable foreign degree. IT specialists can use 3 years' experience instead
Permanent residence
After 27 months, or 21 months with B1 German
Lasts
Your contract plus 6 months, up to 4 years at a time
Official page: Make it in Germany

The EU Blue Card is Germany’s main work permit for graduates, and its rules have become more flexible in recent years. The salary you need is far lower than in the Netherlands, IT specialists can qualify without a degree, and you can get permanent residence in as little as 21 months.

Here’s how it works for applicants from Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Zimbabwe and Zambia.

Who can get one

You need all of these:

  • A degree from a German university, or a foreign degree that’s comparable to a German one. A tertiary qualification of at least three years at level 6 of the European framework can also count.
  • A job offer in Germany for at least 6 months.
  • A job that matches your qualification.
  • A salary that meets the 2026 threshold below.
  • A licence to practise, or the prospect of one, if your profession is regulated, such as doctors and nurses.

You don’t need to speak German for the Blue Card itself, although many employers will ask for it.

For shortage occupations and recent graduates on the lower salary, the Federal Employment Agency must also approve the job.

Shortage occupations

The lower salary applies to jobs including:

  • managers in manufacturing, mining, construction and distribution,
  • ICT service managers,
  • managers in childcare, health and education services,
  • academic STEM professionals, such as engineers and scientists,
  • architects and planners,
  • doctors, vets, dentists and pharmacists,
  • academic nursing and midwifery professionals,
  • teachers and educators.

IT specialists without a degree

You can get a Blue Card without a degree if you have:

  • an IT job offer for at least 6 months,
  • a salary of at least €45,934.20,
  • at least 3 years of IT experience in the last 7 years, at a level comparable to a university graduate.

Check your degree is recognised

Before you apply, check that your university and degree are listed as comparable in the anabin database of the Central Office for Foreign Education (ZAB). If your degree isn’t listed clearly, apply to the ZAB for a Statement of Comparability. Make it in Germany’s recognition finder tells you if your profession needs full recognition too.

How long it lasts, and permanent residence

Your Blue Card lasts for your contract plus 6 months, up to 4 years, and you can extend it in Germany.

You can apply for a settlement permit, which is permanent residence, after:

  • 27 months with basic German (level A1), or
  • 21 months with intermediate German (level B1).

Bringing your family

Your spouse and children can join you. Spouses of Blue Card holders don’t have to prove German before they come, and they can work in Germany.

Changing jobs

You can move to a new job on your Blue Card. If you change jobs in your first year, you must tell your local foreigners authority, which checks you still meet the Blue Card conditions.

How to apply

  1. Get the job offer and a signed contract.
  2. Check your degree is comparable, as above.
  3. Book a national visa appointment at the German embassy or consulate in your country. Many use a visa centre to take applications.
  4. Bring your passport, contract, degree certificates, CV and proof of health insurance.
  5. Once you’re in Germany, register your address and apply for the Blue Card at the foreigners authority.

Your employer can also start the fast-track procedure for skilled workers at the foreigners authority in Germany, which speeds up the embassy appointment and decision.

Blue Card or another route?

  • No job offer yet? The Opportunity Card lets you go to Germany for up to a year to look for one.
  • Vocational qualification instead of a degree? See the other skilled worker routes and Ausbildung.
  • Comparing countries? The Netherlands asks for €5,942 a month for its Blue Card. See the Dutch highly skilled migrant permit.
