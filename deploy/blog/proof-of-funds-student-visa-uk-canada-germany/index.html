Proof of funds for a student visa in 2026: UK, Canada and Germany

Updated 24 Oct 2026 · Facts checked 24 Oct 2026

UK
£1,529 a month in London or £1,171 elsewhere, for up to 9 months, plus first-year fees
Canada
CAD 23,448 for one person (from 1 September 2026), plus tuition and travel
Germany
€11,904 a year, usually in a blocked account

Getting a university offer is only half the journey. To get a student visa, you must also prove you can pay for your studies and your living costs, and each country has its own amount and rules.

Here’s what the UK, Canada and Germany ask for in 2026, and how to prepare your bank statements so they don’t get your visa refused.

UK Student visa

For living costs, you must show:

  • £1,529 a month if you’ll study in London, or
  • £1,171 a month anywhere else in the UK,

for up to 9 months. That’s up to £13,761 for London and £10,539 elsewhere. You also need enough to pay your first year’s course fees, which are shown on your CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies), minus anything you’ve already paid.

The money must have been in your account for at least 28 days in a row, and the end of that 28-day period must be within 31 days of the day you apply. You need extra money for each family member you bring.

If your money is in naira, cedis, shillings or rand, keep a buffer above the minimum, because the amount is checked in pounds and exchange rates move.

Official: GOV.UK, money you need for a Student visa

Canada study permit

For study permit applications received on or after 1 September 2026, you must show CAD 23,448 for one person for a year of living costs, outside Quebec. Applications before that date needed CAD 22,895.

This amount doesn’t include tuition or travel: you must show your first year’s tuition and your travel costs on top. Bringing family raises the amount; IRCC publishes a table by family size. Quebec sets its own amounts.

IRCC accepts several kinds of proof, including:

  • bank statements for the past 6 months, showing where the money came from,
  • a guaranteed investment certificate (GIC) from a Canadian bank,
  • receipts for tuition and housing already paid,
  • a scholarship or student loan letter,
  • a letter from a sponsor, with their job, income and ID.

Official: IRCC, proof of financial support

Germany student visa

Germany expects you to show €11,904 for a year of study, about €992 a month. Most students do this with a blocked account (Sperrkonto): you deposit the full amount before your visa appointment, and once you’re in Germany you can take out a fixed amount each month.

Other accepted proof includes:

  • a scholarship award letter from a recognised scholarship provider,
  • a formal guarantee from someone who lives permanently in Germany,
  • proof of your parents’ income and assets.

Embassies sometimes ask for a different amount, so confirm with the German embassy or consulate in your country before you pay into a blocked account.

Official: Study in Germany (DAAD), proof of financing

Mistakes that get visas refused

  • Money that appears just before you apply. A large deposit with no clear source looks borrowed. Keep the money in the account early, and be ready to show where it came from, such as salary, business income or the sale of property.
  • Statements that don’t meet the rules. Each country has rules on dates, names and stamps. Check them before you print.
  • Sponsors with no proof. If a parent or relative is paying, include their letter, ID, proof of relationship and their own bank statements.
  • Forgetting fees. The UK and Canada both expect tuition on top of living costs.
  • Paying an “agent” to provide funds. Fake or rented statements can get you banned from reapplying. See how to spot fake job and visa offers.

Scholarships change everything

A fully funded scholarship usually counts as proof of funds. See the awards open to Africans in our 2027 scholarship calendar.
