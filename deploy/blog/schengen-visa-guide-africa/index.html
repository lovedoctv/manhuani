Schengen visa for Africans in 2026: fees, documents and how early to apply

Updated 25 Oct 2026 · Facts checked 25 Oct 2026

Fee
€90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6 to 12, plus the visa centre's service fee
When to apply
No earlier than 6 months and no later than 15 days before you travel
Decision
Usually 15 days, up to 45 days
Passport
Valid for at least 3 months after you leave the Schengen area
Official page: European Commission

A Schengen visa is a short-stay visa for visits of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. One visa covers all 29 Schengen countries, including France, Germany, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands and Switzerland. Citizens of Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Zimbabwe and Zambia all need one to visit.

It’s for tourism, family visits, business meetings and conferences. It doesn’t let you work or study long-term. For that you need a national visa from the country, such as Germany’s Opportunity Card.

Which embassy to apply to

  • If you’re visiting one country, apply to that country’s embassy or consulate.
  • If you’re visiting several, apply to the country where you’ll spend the most time.
  • If you’ll spend the same time in each, apply to the country you’ll enter first.

You normally apply in the country where you legally live. Most embassies use a visa centre, such as VFS Global, TLScontact or BLS International, to take applications. Your embassy’s website says which one it uses.

Fees

  • €90 for adults.
  • €45 for children aged 6 to 12.
  • Children under 6 don’t pay the visa fee.

You pay in local currency. The visa centre adds its own service fee on top.

When to apply

You can apply up to 6 months before your trip, and at least 15 days before. Appointments can be hard to get in busy seasons such as summer and December, so book as soon as your dates are firm.

Most decisions take 15 days. If the embassy needs to look more closely or asks for more documents, it can take up to 45 days.

Documents you need

  • A passport valid for at least 3 months after you leave the Schengen area, issued within the last 10 years, with two blank pages.
  • The completed application form.
  • A recent passport photo that meets ICAO standards.
  • Travel medical insurance covering emergency care, hospital treatment and repatriation, with at least €30,000 of cover.
  • Proof of why you’re travelling, such as an invitation letter, a conference registration or a tour booking.
  • Proof of accommodation, such as hotel bookings or a host’s invitation.
  • Proof you can pay for the trip, such as recent bank statements and payslips.
  • Proof you’ll come home, such as a letter from your employer approving your leave, your business registration, or your school enrolment.

You give your fingerprints when you apply, unless you’re exempt. Each embassy can ask for more documents, so always check its own checklist.

Tips that help your application

  • Be consistent. Your bank statements, leave letter and booking dates should all match your travel plan.
  • Show steady money, not a sudden deposit. A large deposit just before you apply raises questions.
  • Never use fake documents. A fake bank statement or booking can get you refused and can lead to a ban. Anyone selling a “guaranteed visa” is a scammer. Read how to spot fake visa offers.
  • Build a travel history. People who used past visas properly are more likely to get longer, multiple-entry visas later.

At the border

Since 10 April 2026, the EU’s Entry/Exit System (EES) records your fingerprints, photo and entry and exit dates at the border instead of stamping your passport. It keeps count of your 90 days, so don’t overstay.

You may also hear about ETIAS. It’s a travel authorisation for people who don’t need a visa, such as Americans. If you need a Schengen visa, you don’t need ETIAS.
